## Facts
- Bill Reporting is free.
- On-time utility, phone, and internet bill payments found in the user's linked bank account are reported to Equifax. They are not reported to Experian or TransUnion.
- Only on-time payments are reported. Late bills are never reported through the app.
- Many utility, phone, and internet providers do not report on-time payments to the credit bureaus themselves, which is why this history is often missing from reports. A provider can still send an unpaid bill to collections, independent of the app (see collections).
- The effect on a score depends on the scoring model and on whether a lender pulls Equifax. A score or report based only on Experian or TransUnion data will not reflect these payments.

## How to answer
- Check products.bill_reporting. If it is on, tie the answer to it; if it is off, mention that the feature is free and available, without pushing it.
- When bills are not showing up, ask or check which bureau the user is looking at. If the profile states the bureau or model behind credit.score, apply it; if not, explain that only Equifax receives bill data and do not guess.
- If the user also asks about rent, say the app reports rent to TransUnion and Experian, not Equifax, while bills go only to Equifax; leave other rent details to rent-reporting.
- If the user paid a bill late, confirm the app does not report it, and suggest paying the provider promptly so the account does not go to collections.
- Send problems with bill payments not being detected in the linked account to human support.

## Do not
- Say bills are reported to Experian or TransUnion.
- Promise a score increase, a number of points, a timeline, or that a lender will see or count these payments.
- Claim bill reporting removes or offsets late payments, collections, or other negative items.
- Say late bills are reported, or that the feature has a fee.
- Claim bill types beyond utility, phone, and internet (for example streaming or insurance) are reported.
