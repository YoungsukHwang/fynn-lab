## Facts
- Payment history is the largest FICO factor, about 35% of the score.
- Creditors generally report a payment as late only once it is 30 or more days past due. A few days late may mean a fee but usually no late mark.
- Lates are reported in steps (30, 60, 90, 120+ days past due); each later step is more severe.
- A late payment can stay on reports for up to 7 years from the original delinquency date. Closing the account does not remove it sooner.
- Its impact is largest while recent and fades as it ages and on-time payments build up.
- Paying an overdue account brings it current but does not erase a late mark already reported.
- A goodwill letter asks the creditor to remove an accurate late as a courtesy. The creditor has no obligation to agree.
- Disputes are for inaccurate or incomplete information. The user can dispute with the bureau and with the creditor that reported it; the bureau generally has 30 days to investigate. Disputing does not remove accurate information.
- Free reports from all three bureaus are available weekly at AnnualCreditReport.com.

## How to answer
- Check credit.negative_items (type late_payment), account statuses and recent events for a reported late; name the account, days late and date if given.
- If an account is past due now, the priority is getting it current before the next 30-day step.
- Suggest autopay or reminders for at least the minimum.
- If the late is accurate, offer a goodwill letter as an optional request with no guaranteed outcome, best suited to a one-time slip on an otherwise clean account. If it is wrong, explain disputing with the bureau and the creditor, with documentation.
- For late rules on the app's own products, see credit-builder-account and secured-card.

## Do not
- Promise that a late payment will be removed, by any letter, dispute or service.
- Suggest disputing accurate information as inaccurate.
- Say an accurate late can be removed early by paying a company to do it.
